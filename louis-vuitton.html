The Graffiti Défilé.

Louis Vuitton is about to spray graffiti on its oldest icon. Tomorrow morning you tell the product committee what to launch, at what price, in what quantity, and where.

The situation.

An invitation to the show, a bold artistic idea, and less than a day to turn it into a commercial plan.

For most people, the invitation to the next Louis Vuitton show (this year it arrives as a T-shirt) is a treat. For Emmanuel Lesac, group manager for leather goods, it marks the start of the most hectic days of the year. Only at the show will he see which of the leather goods prototypes creative director Marc Jacobs has chosen for the catwalk.

The show presents the Spring/Summer ready-to-wear and shoe collection, but the leather goods are expected to steal it. Jacobs has asked the American underground artist Stephen Sprouse to paint graffiti across icon pieces of the Monogram canvas, the house's most classic line, created in 1896 and the visual symbol of Louis Vuitton itself. The idea is revolutionary. It could also divide Louis Vuitton's management and its existing customers.

Every product exists only as a prototype. Right after the show, the leather goods marketing team prepares its recommendations for the product committee on 12 October. Whatever the committee approves goes into production and, if all goes well, reaches the stores in March. Both the commercial result and the brand image are at stake.

You are the marketing representative at that committee. The full case text is in the course files on Google Drive. The case was written by Marc Vanhuele (HEC Paris) with Louis Vuitton.

Seven decisions.

For the committee, you propose what goes to market and how. Enter every decision in the decision sheet.

  1. Which lines?Commercialise all five lines, some, or none.
  2. Which products and colours?Within each line, choose the products and colours, and the share of each colour. You may propose changes to the prototypes.
  3. Retail priceSet a price per product. Use the cost and the price of the products that inspired each line.
  4. QuantityProduction capacity is fixed. The quantities of the inspiring products are a reference point.
  5. StoresFour store types: flagship; ready-to-wear, shoes and leather goods; shoes and leather goods; leather goods only.
  6. Time in storesOne season (six months), several seasons, or permanent.
  7. Press focusWhich products get special attention in press briefings to generate editorials?

Reading the costs

That 2.0x multiplier is the benchmark the case gives you. Do you keep it for an artist collaboration, or does the Graffiti collection justify a different price logic?

What you bring to class.

The committee wants the numbers and the reasons behind them.

1 · The numbers

The completed decision sheet

Every launched product with its colours, colour shares, price, quantity, stores, time in stores and press focus. The Summary checks should all read zero.

2 · The rationale

A short slide deck

Your recommendation to the product committee, and why. Lead with the decision, then the arguments: brand, customer, price, capacity, channel and press. If you use a GenAI tool, keep your prompts in the slide notes, as the course AI policy requires.

Think it through.

Questions to structure your rationale with the course frameworks.

Brand

Renewal or dilution?

Graffiti on the 1896 Monogram can refresh the brand or unsettle its core customers. Which products carry the risk, and which carry the reward?

Customers

Who buys graffiti?

Is the target the existing Monogram customer, a younger fashion-led buyer, or both? What does each segment value, and what would push each one away?

Price

Keep the 2.0x rule?

In luxury, price signals value. Does an artist collaboration deserve a premium over the inspiring product, the same multiplier, or a lower entry point for the small pieces?

Scarcity

One season or permanent?

Limited time and limited quantity create desire, but leave money on the table. Which pieces should be rare, and which could become new classics?

Place

Where should it be seen?

Distribution is positioning. A plexiglass Nightclubber in every store sends a different signal from one in the flagships only.

Capacity and press

What gets made, and what gets shown?

Capacity is fixed: every Graffiti bag may replace a classic one. Which products sell, which make headlines, and are they the same products?
