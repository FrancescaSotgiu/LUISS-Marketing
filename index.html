Optional extra · AI and marketing

The human in the loop decides.

An AI agent can write a hundred posts an hour. It cannot tell whether a claim is true, whether a discount damages the brand, or whether a message lands in another culture. That judgement is marketing.

The exercise

  1. Write the brief. One page for your project brand: positioning statement, target, tone of voice, facts the agent may use, and red lines it must never cross.
  2. Build the agent. Any assistant you can configure with instructions and files (a custom GPT, a Claude Project, a Gemini Gem).
  3. Give it three tasks. A launch post, a reply to an unhappy customer, and a promotion proposal.
  4. Check the outputs. Score each one on the five checks, correct it, and log what you changed and why.
  5. Use it. Report what the agent got wrong in one slide of your presentation, and keep the prompts in the slide notes as the AI policy requires.

Five checks

TruthIs every fact in the brief or a cited source?
ClaimsIs any environmental or origin claim specific and verifiable?
Brand fitDoes it support the positioning, at the level of values, not only attributes?
ValueDoes it protect price positioning and customer lifetime value?
ContextDoes it work for this market, culture and channel?

Group project · 40%

A strategic marketing plan.

In teams, build a marketing plan for a real or fictional brand in a sector you choose. You start in class in every lesson and continue at home. In Lesson 4 you present it in 10 minutes and defend it in the Q&A.

What the plan covers

  • STP summary: segments, target and positioning, with a positioning map.
  • Brand identity and value proposition: why your target chooses you, at the level of benefits and values.
  • 4 Ps plan with KPIs: product, price, place and promotion as one programme, with objectives and targets.
  • Insights from data: the market insights and sources behind your choices.
  • GenAI prompts: every prompt you used, in the slide notes.

Choosing your brand

Launch a new brand or fix a marketing problem of an existing one. A good project names a brand, a decision and a trade-off. Some starting points:

  • Should a heritage luxury house launch its own resale and repair service?
  • How should a European premium food or beverage brand position itself to enter Japan or the US?
  • Can a young luxury label grow without losing exclusivity?
  • How can a fashion brand prove that its supply chain is fair and communicate it with claims that are specific and verifiable?
